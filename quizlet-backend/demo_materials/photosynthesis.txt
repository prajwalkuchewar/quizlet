Photosynthesis Basics

Photosynthesis is the process plants, algae, and some bacteria use to convert light energy into chemical energy. The energy is stored as glucose.

The overall equation is: 6 CO2 + 6 H2O + light energy -> C6H12O6 + 6 O2.

Photosynthesis happens mainly in the chloroplasts, which contain the green pigment chlorophyll. Chlorophyll absorbs mostly blue and red light and reflects green light, which is why leaves look green.

The process has two stages:
1. Light-dependent reactions occur in the thylakoid membranes. Water is split, oxygen is released, and ATP and NADPH are produced.
2. The Calvin cycle occurs in the stroma. It uses ATP and NADPH to fix carbon dioxide into sugar.

Factors that limit the rate of photosynthesis include light intensity, carbon dioxide concentration, and temperature.
